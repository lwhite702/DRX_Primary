This guide is your store's daily operating playbook for keeping physical stock counts and digital records in sync.

## What this page is for

Use this guide to keep all product movements fully traceable, avoid unexpected count discrepancies, and ensure your store records are always ready for internal or external review.

It covers best practices for receiving stock shipments, logging damaged goods, and writing clear adjustment notes.

## Before you start

- **Recommended role**:
  - Cashier: Check shelf counts and report count discrepancies.
  - Supervisor or Manager: Log stock adjustments, review damaged goods, and inspect audit logs.
- **Key screens**:
  - **Receive Stock** (`/inventory/intake`): Record supplier shipments and invoices.
  - **Adjust Stock** (`/inventory/adjust`): Log recount corrections and shrinkage.
  - **Stock Audit Trail** (`/inventory/audit`): View the timestamped history of every count change.
- **Store rule**:
  - Every manual stock correction must include a clear explanation that any other team member can easily understand.

## Fast path

1. **Log all incoming inventory immediately** (`/inventory/intake`):
   - Never place items directly on sales shelves without logging an intake shipment first.
2. **Submit count adjustments when variances are found** (`/inventory/adjust`):
   - Select the product, choose **Increase**, **Decrease**, or **Set Count to**, and enter the new physical count.
3. **Log damaged or lost items under shrinkage**:
   - Never hide lost, broken, or expired products inside vague notes. Log them explicitly with clear details.
4. **Inspect your audit trail daily** (`/inventory/audit`):
   - Review recent adjustments to see who made changes and ensure reason notes are descriptive.
5. **Investigate recurring discrepancies**:
   - If a specific item requires repeat adjustments across multiple shifts, notify store management to investigate shelf handling or packaging practices.

## If blocked

- **Cannot submit a stock adjustment**:
  - Check that you selected a valid adjustment type and entered an updated quantity.
  - Verify that your account has supervisor or manager permissions to adjust stock.
- **Adjustment reason note is too short**:
  - Write a helpful note explaining what happened, why the count changed, and who verified it.
- **Spot-check reveals a shelf count mismatch**:
  - Pause sales on that specific shelf section.
  - Do a complete physical recount, submit a single clear adjustment note, and resume selling.
- **Audit trail does not show a recent change**:
  - Check that the adjustment was saved and verify that you are viewing the correct store location.

## Good examples for adjustment notes

Write specific, informative reasons:

- ✅ *"Recount of shelf 4 after morning restocking (+5 units, verified by Maria)"*
- ✅ *"Dropped bottle during unboxing (-1 unit, discarded)"*
- ✅ *"Corrected vendor intake entry to match posted supplier invoice"*

Avoid vague, unhelpful notes:

- ❌ *"fix"*
- ❌ *"update"*
- ❌ *"count changed"*

## New staff training habits

- Teach new cashiers how to report count discrepancies on day one.
- Show supervisors how to review previous adjustments before correcting a count a second time.
- Emphasize that finding and documenting a discrepancy right away protects the entire store team.
