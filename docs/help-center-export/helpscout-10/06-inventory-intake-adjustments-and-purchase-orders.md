## Create a product

Open **Inventory** and add the product name, price, and any SKU or barcode you use. Keep identifiers unique and confirm the active workspace before saving.

## Receive stock

1. Open **Inventory intake**.
2. Choose the correct product.
3. Enter the received quantity.
4. Add unit cost, vendor, batch, and reference details you track.
5. Review every line.
6. Submit or approve the intake according to your role.
7. Confirm the on-hand count and audit entry.

Use a new intake for a delivery. Do not use an adjustment to hide a missing receiving record.

## Make a count adjustment

Use **Inventory adjustment** for damage, shrink, cycle-count corrections, returns, or another approved reason.

1. Confirm the product and workspace.
2. Enter the correct quantity or change requested by the form.
3. Choose a clear reason.
4. Add a short note with enough context for review.
5. Save once and confirm the result in the audit trail.

## Low-stock alerts

Low-stock signals help the team decide what to receive or order next. Confirm the live on-hand count, recent sales, and pending intake before acting on an alert.

## Costs, vendors, and batches

Record cost and vendor details during intake when your business tracks them. Batch information helps the team trace units from the same delivery. These records provide operational context; they are not certified accounting output.

## Purchase orders

Purchase-order tracking is beta. Use it only for the workflow available in your workspace.

- Confirm products, quantities, vendor, and expected date.
- Treat the purchase order as planned stock, not on-hand inventory.
- Receive the actual delivery through Inventory intake.
- Record differences between ordered and received quantities.
- Confirm the final on-hand count and audit trail.

Do not advertise a complete purchasing or vendor-management system while this area remains beta.

## Common mistakes

- Receiving into the wrong workspace
- Using an adjustment instead of intake
- Reusing another product's barcode
- Saving without a reason
- Treating a purchase order as received stock
- Retrying after a delayed save without checking the audit trail

## After every stock change

Check the product count, audit trail, low-stock status, and any related cost or batch details. If the result looks wrong, stop and use the inventory troubleshooting guide before making another change.
