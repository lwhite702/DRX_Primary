---
title: "Inventory audit trail and stock troubleshooting"
description: "Trace stock changes, investigate mismatches, and correct counts without hiding history."
---

## Use the audit trail

Open **Inventory audit** to see when stock changed, which user acted, the quantity, and the recorded reason. Use it before making a second change.

## Investigate a count mismatch

1. Confirm the workspace and product.
2. Check the current on-hand count.
3. Review recent sales, intake, adjustments, refunds, and other movements.
4. Compare the physical count with the system count.
5. Identify the first point where they differ.
6. Ask a Manager or Admin to approve the correction when required.
7. Make one reasoned adjustment.
8. Confirm the new count and audit entry.

Never delete, overwrite, or disguise the original movement.

## Intake did not change stock

- Confirm the intake was submitted or approved.
- Check the product and workspace.
- Look for the intake in the audit trail.
- Refresh once before retrying.
- If no movement exists, capture the intake reference and exact message.

## An adjustment looks duplicated

Do not add another offset until you confirm both entries. Record the timestamps, quantities, users, and reasons. Ask an Admin to choose the approved correction.

## A product is missing at checkout

- Confirm it is active and belongs to the current workspace.
- Check on-hand stock.
- Search by name or SKU.
- Verify the barcode mapping.
- Review role restrictions and inventory status.

## Low-stock alert looks wrong

Confirm the live count, threshold, recent sales, pending intake, and workspace. A recent movement may require a refresh before the alert clears.

## Cost, vendor, or batch data is wrong

Check the original intake first. Use the approved correction path and keep a clear note. Do not change stock merely to correct a descriptive field unless the workflow requires it.

## Escalation details

Send support the workspace, product name or SKU, current count, expected count, movement timestamps, and exact error. Screenshots should exclude customer data and secrets.

## Safe correction rule

One verified mismatch should lead to one approved correction and one clear audit reason. If you are not sure which movement is wrong, stop and ask an Admin.
