# DRX User Manual

You are here to keep shifts moving, keep customers happy, and keep stock clean.

Use this for quick decisions and fewer mistakes.

## Who this is for

- Owners
- Managers
- Operators
- Support agents

## Start in 30 seconds

1. Pick your role document.
2. Open [DRX Admin Manual](./drx-admin-manual.md) if you manage permissions or approvals.
3. Open [DRX Staff Manual](./drx-staff-manual.md) if you run day-to-day operations.
4. Open [Customer Service Playbook](./customer-service-playbook.md) when you are helping customers.
5. Confirm workspace and location before any save or approval.

## Core rules

- One workspace only.
- Confirm what changed before you save.
- Keep money and stock tied together.
- Write notes short: action + result.

## Start-of-shift checklist

1. Sign in.
2. Confirm workspace and location in the top header.
3. Open Dashboard and read top alerts.
4. Pick one main focus: checkout, inventory, reports, or support.
5. Make sure no carts are left open from before.

## Before checkout

1. Check printer and scanner.
2. Confirm workspace location.
3. Add one item by scan or search, then confirm it appears.
4. Verify discounts and taxes are correct for your store.

## End-of-shift checklist

1. Confirm no open carts remain.
2. Match Sales and Reports for your shift time window.
3. Review pending approvals and escalations.
4. Leave one handoff note for anything unresolved.

## Help a customer without stopping the line

1. Ask for one sentence: what happened and what should have happened.
2. Confirm workspace and the exact flow they used.
3. Recreate once in the same place.
4. Use the right playbook path: checkout, inventory, or access.
5. If policy blocks it, escalate right away with details.

## What to send when escalating

- Workspace and location
- Time and timezone
- User and action
- Where it happened
- Expected result
- Actual result
- Screenshot or exact error text

## Keep manuals current

When your flow changes, update:

- Admin Manual
- Staff Manual
- Customer Service Playbook

Keep updates short and practical.
